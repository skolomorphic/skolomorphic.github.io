Given that Thais had specifically named the Farzan family as one of their opponents, I wasn’t surprised that they’d try to get in the way, but I hadn’t expected Nastaran to approach to me directly.

“How did you know I was here?”

“The Duval family isn’t the only one with a stake in this hotel,” Nastaran replied. “Try the coffee — it’s a specialty imported Saivanian brand.”

I took the folder and glanced through the contents — largely legal documents and financial reports that threatened to give me a headache. “And why this company?”

“Haven’t you been trying to take over it for ages?” Nastaran gave me a knowing smile. “Their licenses and papers have just been renewed, and they have a good reputation; the cargo inspections for them will be quite loose for at least a year, I’d say.”

As best as I could tell, she was bribing me with a smuggling company that had authorization to operate in her territory. Just what had the Duval family been up to, that she’d think this would work on me?

“Let me think on this,” I said, closing the folder. “If I agree, I’ll need time to verify the information here.”

“But of course,” Nastaran said with a smile. “Don’t take too long, though — if the situation changes, the offer will, too.”

“A merchant through and through, aren’t you?”

“Why, thank you. I’m so glad we could come to a preliminary accord.”

I didn’t plan on keeping this identity forever, so all the power and privilege that came with it was temporary anyway, but I should be more careful about how I treated others from here on out. It seemed like it’d be even easier to accidentally punch down on someone than I’d previously realized.

If I compared my current position to a quantum territory owner in my old world, then it was pretty amazing that anyone outside that circle would be willing to talk to me at all, let alone do something like ask me for help. Unfortunately, not everything could be understood in terms of what I knew.

There wasn’t much I could do to advance my plans but wait, so I spent my day at the department reading through a book about the Hierarchs.

What I learned was that the Hierarchs were neither invincible nor immortal, though they did not appear to have a natural limit to their lifespan. Sometimes, a Hierarch simply vanished from history, though all known causes of a Hierarch’s end were due to being killed. Such a slaying was considered a Legend-level feat.

At any given time, there were no more than seven Dragons and no more than three Phoenixes in the world; should one of them to perish, another Dragon or Phoenix would eventually appear and take the position of its predecessor. The current-day Dragon of Rage, for example, had emerged around five hundred years ago, after the Dragon of Hatred disappeared; the current Dragon of Sorrow was speculated to be between nine hundred and thirteen hundred years of age, following the slaying of the Dragon of Lamentation.

The Scribe’s Chimeras were far more mysterious and elusive. It was uncertain how many of them there were, or if they had a limit to their numbers the way the other Hierarchs did. It was speculated, however, that a Chimera generally emerged after the concurrent death or disappearance of two or more Hierarchs. As such, a common — though unproven — theory said that the Chimeras were created out of dead Hierarchs. It was also speculated that a Chimera’s birth was correlated with Kosmonymia’s infamous hidden periods of history.

That, combined with what I’d learned from Thais about “previous iterations” of the First Singer’s stories, didn’t give me a great feeling.

If `[Bound Prophet]` wasn’t a title inherited from past prophets, then Acacius had to have been a prophet before he received the title. He seemed concerned with visions of the end of the world. Had he gotten glimpses of “previous iterations,” somehow? But wouldn’t that count more as seeing the past than seeing the future?

I didn’t know how the Tripartite decided these things. If I had the opportunity in the future, should I try asking…?

Maybe after I got answers about my own world, first.

It was another quiet day at the office, so I started reading through a book Miss Bakhta had recommended to me at the library, titled Myths and Legends in Antiquity. It gathered together historical records on mythological figures, and there was a whole chapter dedicated to Melchior and the `[Red Flood]` incident.

Historical sources largely corroborated the Anthemoessians’ version of things; Melchior had stayed with one of their clans for some length of time before returning to his kingdom with an unprecedented new power, taking it over and launching a bloody crusade across three continents.

Non-Anthemoessian historical records speculated that Melchior had learned a secret and powerful blood art from the Anthemoessians, which the author noted had resulted in malice and prejudice towards them throughout the centuries: “Doubtless these rumors are false, or the Anthemoessians would have rained down bloody terror on anyone who attempted to persecute them in the days afterwards.”

Melchior hadn’t been very forthcoming about the reasons for his war, relying mostly on might and terror to drive his army forward, but the people who’d survived under his command had guessed that the war was in some way the price for Melchior’s newfound power.

It was recorded that the seas had turned red, and that people believed it to be a title spirit of Melchior’s; it was widely uncontested that the Dragon of Hatred had killed him. However, regarding the Bright One and the origins of the Ritual of the Buried Storm, there was no mention.

The Anthemoessians had been willing to share the story with me, so I could only conclude that the reason why this author hadn’t included it was because they hadn’t asked or hadn’t deemed it legitimate enough to include in the book. I guess that made the prevailing cultural attitude towards the Anthemoessians clear.

It was something I found hard to understand, as an outsider, but there were plenty of race and class divisions in my world that likely would’ve felt absurd to outsiders, too. I’d just have to do my best to be mindful of how my current social identity related to them.
